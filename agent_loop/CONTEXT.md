## Known facts and open problems

- Toronto (Collins et al. 2015 large beam, no stirrups in the east span; test 685 kN incl. self-weight, east-span shear failure): the model is run without self-weight; the test curve is shifted by the self-weight deflection 1.008 mm; the no-self-weight failure load should be about 775–950 kN (pass range 700–1050). The model's elastic stiffness (342 kN/mm Coarse, 327 Medium) agrees with beam theory (~320); the test is ~1.5x softer (support/bearing deformation) — do not chase the initial slope. The shear stress on east-span crack planes stays small (≤ 0.75 MPa at 681 kN) and crack openings are small: the constant retained crack shear β G is NOT what carries the extra load. No version so far shows a shear failure: the diagonal crack never localises into a failure mechanism (brittle cracking, no softening of the crack shear with opening, cap never reached).
- Axially loaded walls (SW16, SW22): brittle crushing at the compressed toe at ~65 % of the load was the problem of the crushing rules; the strut cap removes it but SW16 Medium does not converge at step 25.
- W-2 Coarse is 31 % too strong while Medium is +4 %: the coarse panel mesh over-predicts the strut capacity (mesh dependence).
- Head (loading beam) elements of the Lefas walls crack at the first step in the axially loaded walls (the axial load is a body force of the head); this is a modelling artefact and is only a warning.
- Slab strips (out-of-plane) are new: never run before the loop. Expect issues with layered cracking (each layer cracks independently) and the brittle tension (no tension stiffening, same as the analytical reference).
- Convergence pattern of the native NR: residual stays ~0.1–0.4 while crack states change, then drops to ~1e-9 in one iteration once the crack pattern is stable.
